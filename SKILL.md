# Joint Pursuit Framework

Use JPF to keep a joint proposal fast, attributable, and deliverable. Treat AI as a drafting and reconciliation aid, never as commitment authority.

## Start Here

1. Read [README.md](README.md) for the public overview and minimum workflow.
2. Copy the files in `templates/` into the pursuit workspace.
3. Establish one authoritative register. Do not let email, chat, meeting summaries, or separate drafts silently become competing sources of truth.
4. Enforce the three gates below.

## Three Required Gates

### 1. Charter and data-use gate

Before substantive drafting, identify the prime or client lead, bid manager, participants, communication authority, commercial decision owners, information-sharing rules, and withdrawal path. Record unresolved items explicitly.

### 2. Requirement and delivery-ownership gate

Before persuasive language is treated as approved, trace each material requirement to its source and give every promised outcome one accountable delivery owner. Distinguish company capability, individual experience, coordinated capability, and proven joint delivery. Never turn the first three into the fourth.

### 3. Submission freeze and closeout gate

Before submission, verify claims, permissions, estimates, responsibilities, and approvals; then freeze the exact submitted package. After the decision, record the outcome and either transition commitments into delivery or apply the agreed retention rules.

## Operating Rules

- Preserve the owner, source, approval state, and limitations of every material claim.
- Separate vendor documentation, demonstration evidence, planned configuration, custom work, and proven implementation.
- Use one accountable owner per outcome. “Joint” or “shared” may describe collaboration, but not accountability.
- Treat meeting transcripts and AI summaries as leads to verify, not authoritative decisions.
- Label assumptions, unresolved conflicts, and missing approvals instead of smoothing them into proposal prose.
- Require named human approval for disclosures, compliance assertions, staffing, pricing, contractual positions, delivery commitments, and final submission.
- Minimize cross-company disclosure. Prefer bounded questions and approved excerpts over bulk access to another participant's knowledge base.
- Do not imply permission to send messages, submit a proposal, share restricted material, or commit another party.

## Minimum Workflow

1. Create the pursuit charter from `templates/01-pursuit-charter.md`.
2. Create the authoritative register from `templates/02-authoritative-register.md` and populate one row per material requirement or commitment.
3. Use `templates/03-partner-request.md` only when a register item needs information or approval from another participant.
4. Draft proposal language only from register items whose evidence and approval state support the wording.
5. Run `templates/04-submission-and-closeout.md`; preserve a frozen submission manifest.

For detailed vocabulary, evidence classes, and review checks, read [references/framework.md](references/framework.md). For a safe demonstration, use [examples/fictional-pursuit.md](examples/fictional-pursuit.md).

## Expected Outputs

- Completed pursuit charter
- Current authoritative register
- Bounded partner requests for unresolved items, if needed
- Proposal language with attributable claims and visible qualifications
- Submission manifest and closeout or delivery-transition record

This framework is an experimental governance method, not legal advice or a substitute for an NDA, teaming agreement, subcontract, or procurement counsel.
